Bloc Fantôme 2.9.5: two-scene sanctuary presentation

Approved direction
Doorway click starts the cinematic. The camera shifts the shrine left with a modest pullback, retaining its visible
glowstone. The cathedral emerges through a clipped void plane at upper right,
initially partly cropped. The shrine lowers while its glowstone withdraws upward,
and the camera tracks the rising sanctuary into its final close composition. Two detached, bay-aligned arcades and paired
stepped lantern towers frame the nave. Ivory stone, muted blue glass, amber light.
The third monument and Redstone Map development remain deferred.

Behavior
Place the ghost stone, click the revealed doorway, then touch the glass cross.
The altar is also interactive when visible from the current angle. Other marked
piers give a light/sound response. Return restores the previous world, camera,
window dimensions and undo history. Escape or the upper-right X also exits.
The opening uses borderless fullscreen; --opening-windowed is the testing option.
F11 toggles only the presentation display. Continuous Q/E orbit and the slider
unlock after the first reveal and pause during the cinematic. Settings contains
replay and the persisted skip-on-launch toggle.

Presentation and fixes
Native-resolution textured geometry and native-size UI, using a bounded OpenGL
presentation renderer. The editor renderer remains unchanged. The software
fallback uses the same camera and motion but flat-colored faces.
Sparse hover/rotation/chime sounds, a smooth low-frequency stone rumble with audible harmonics and grinding cues, and
restrained screen tremor accompany the movement. The owner-selected piano is
retained. Audio cue execution was checked; no subjective listening claim.
The full swamp painting fits its startup window, with a small title over a
lower-edge vignette. Only the native window caption contains [2.9.5].
Small tutorial subjects now use explicit subject bounds; subjects sit above the hotbar. The tower bounds were
corrected against actual captured block coordinates after visual inspection.
A retained pygame SDL Window wrapper prevents a native crash after returning
from the OpenGL presentation to the software editor.
