Research and design decisions, 2026-10-20

Intent
Strengthen the existing architectural opening, its camera and interaction, while
retaining the creature and the owner's Minecraft materials. These are design
judgments informed by sources, not proof that one formula makes every game good.

Monument Valley
Ken Wong's direct interviews put architecture and the integrated experience at
the center. Optical illusions are a tool, not its entire identity. His described
workflow starts with one readable geometric idea, playable blockout and user
testing, then an art pass. Each scene contains the information needed to act.
Our application: give the shrine, threshold, nave and terminal glass distinct
roles; preserve empty space between masses; make the next action visible without
adding more HUD. Avoid importing a pastel palette or inventing a perspective
puzzle merely to resemble the reference.

Cathedral architecture
The V&A describes rib vaults, external buttresses, pointed arches and tracery as
connected structural and visual devices. Chartres's own account emphasizes the
balance of vertical and horizontal lines, changing light, processional space,
and the differing character of its windows. The design lesson is a legible
hierarchy of large masses, repeated bays, then small details, with light giving
the focal point meaning. More ornament everywhere would obscure that hierarchy.
Our application: four diagonal ribs converge at each bay boss, attached shafts
continue the vertical rhythm, paired clerestory lights get small circular
tracery. Retain the open cutaway and warm terminal cross so both interior and
interaction remain visible. This is a fictional sanctuary, not a reconstruction.

Camera, cinematics and emotional rhythm
Haigh-Hutchinson treats camera presentation as fundamental to interaction, with
film principles adapted to the player's changing needs. The current cross click
reset both orbit and zoom immediately, breaking spatial continuity. The revision
starts from the player's exact camera and eases into the encounter shot. The
arrival pan reaches a readable cathedral composition before the rise ends.
Booth's Left 4 Dead presentation distinguishes build-up, peak, fade and relief;
constant intensity is fatiguing. That is evidence for contrast, not a reason to
copy an adaptive combat director into this small opening. Retain quiet inspection
before the voluntary trigger and a finite encounter afterward. Naughty Dog's
production account also demonstrates that convincing motion needs blocking,
performance, cleanup, lighting and repeated revision, not just a loaded rig.
The creature remains intact for the owner's review. Future removal should be an
explicit artistic decision, not cleanup or a hidden fallback.

UI/UX and latency
Microsoft's objective-clarity guidance supports recognizable next actions and
optional reminders. Nielsen's response-time guidance explains why long silent
loads interrupt attention. Our minimal opening UI remains Q/E; wheel still
zooms. The existing tutorial overlay remains movable/minimizable. The expensive
lighting calculation is moved into prepared lesson data or worker staging,
with exact-result verification and normal invalidation after edits. No fake
percentage or artificially delayed animation masks the load.

Research limits and visual comparison
Read direct interviews, museum/cathedral accounts, camera and Valve presentation
PDFs, and official UX guidance. GDC session descriptions were read; their full
talk videos were not watched. Inspected an actual Monument Valley gameplay image
and a Chartres facade photograph against the existing native cathedral/arrival
renders. Sources and local copies are in .qa/architecture. The scene comparison
focuses on silhouette, bays, negative space, focal contrast and action visibility,
not polygon count. Later rendering and test results belong in the release record.

Cleanup boundaries
Remove only enumerated untracked generated output: older installers (keep the
newest two), PyInstaller work, Python bytecode and pytest fixture directories.
Resolve absolute paths, reject linked paths, and protect Git-tracked files.
Keep user saves/settings, creature assets, licensed source libraries, source
capture worlds, prior visual evidence and research. File count alone cannot
establish that a dynamically loaded texture, sound or reference is unused.
